Issue (1):

Critical load-bearing capacity of beams in the Auditorium, due to larger spans. 

What is the cause?
Significantly larger spans of the beams than in the rest of the building. Therefore, the utilisation is the highest in the beams at the auditoriums, making them the governing subject when evaluating possible future extensions of the building.
The team acknowledged this, however their tool is only considering the Ultimate Limit State design, meanwhile Serviceability Limit State design could also be governing in terms of the beam's utilisation.

Whats the potential **solution** from each of these perspectives?

Design:
By realising the full utility of the beams, a plan for an extension can be evaluated. 

Tool:
A tool that not only considers ULS but also SLS design of beams.

Issue (2):

Critical load-bearing capacity of columns in the Zone 1.

What is the cause?
Several columns in Zone 1 are already subjected to high loads under existing conditions. The building expansion will increase axial forces and bending moments, which could exceed their remaining capacity and pose a design problem. Furthermore, the available results are based on historical calculations and do not verify the columns according to current Eurocode requirements.

What are the possible solutions?

Design:
Use a lightweight structural system, redistribute the additional loads using new vertical elements, or reinforce critical columns and foundations.

Modeling:
Develop an updated structural model that includes the proposed floors, current load combinations, column-foundation interaction, and second-order effects.

Issue (3):

Removal of load-bearing walls impacts global stability.

What is the cause?
Slender concrete walls are present at the auditoriums. By removing these walls, dead load can be lowered in order to achieve a lower utilisation of the beams. A structural analysis is required to determine whether these slender walls are contributing to the overall stability of the structure.


Whats the potential **solution** from each of these perspectives?

Design:
Analyse the global stability of the structure without the walls, and evaluate their performance. Based on their performance, remove all or some of the slender concrete walls.

Issue (4):

Unacceptably ratio of Type f foundations.

What is the cause?
The Type F foundations already have a 99% ratio, leaving virtually no capacity for the additional loads resulting from the planned building extension. Furthermore, the original calculations used safety factors from 1961, which are less conservative than current Eurocode requirements. Therefore, the proposed additional stores could exceed the bearing capacity of these foundations, creating a significant design problem.

What are the possible solutions?

Design:
Avoid transferring additional loads from the extension to the Type F foundations, redistribute them to foundations with greater remaining capacity, or reinforce and extend the affected foundations.

Modeling:
Develop an updated foundation model that incorporates the loads from the building extension, the current soil conditions, and potential differential settlements.

Tools:
Use or develop a tool that evaluates existing foundations with the proposed expansion according to current Eurocode requirements, including ultimate limit state bearing capacity and settlement verification.

Issue (5):

Finding load-reserves by comparing the original loads with the current loads. 

What is the cause?
Load reserves can be found for all elements by comparing the loads applied in the original calculations with the currently present loads on the structure.Currently there is no tool preforming such analysis.

Whats the potential **solution** from each of these perspectives?

Design:
By finding load reserves, details for a possible extension can be established, such as location and extent. Furthermore, other changes in the structural system can be assessed and optimised.

Tool:
A tool that can compare the original loads with the current loads and provide the load reserves for all floors.
